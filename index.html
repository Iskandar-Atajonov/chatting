01 — Core ideas

Four ideas that break intuition

Everyday objects have one place, one speed and one story. Quantum objects don't. Each of these ideas has been tested again and again, and it has always held.

01 / SUPERPOSITION

Many states at once

A system can exist as a blend of several states at once, until a measurement picks one.

02 / ENTANGLEMENT

Linked at any distance

Two particles can share a single state, so measuring one is correlated with the other at any distance.

03 / WAVE–PARTICLE DUALITY

Waves that land as dots

Light and matter produce interference patterns like waves, yet land as single points like particles.

04 / UNCERTAINTY

Pin one, lose the other

Position and momentum cannot both be known exactly. The limit is Δx·Δp ≥ ħ/2. Squeeze one and the other spreads.

02 — Experiment

The double-slit, live

Particles are fired one at a time. Each one lands as a single dot, but together they paint stripes, as if every particle went through both slits. Turn on the detector to see which slit each one uses, and the stripes disappear.

03 — Constants

The numbers behind it

Three constants set the scale of the quantum world and how fast anything can move through it.

h
6.626 × 10−34J·s

Planck's constant, the scale of the quantum world.

c
299 792 458m/s

The speed of light in vacuum, exact by definition.

ħ
1.055 × 10−34J·s

The reduced Planck constant, ħ = h / 2π.

04 — History

From a hack to a theory

It started as a mathematical trick to fix one formula. Within thirty years it had become the most precisely tested theory in science.

  1. 1900

    Energy comes in packets

    Max Planck proposes that energy is exchanged in discrete quanta.

  2. 1905

    Light is made of quanta

    Einstein explains the photoelectric effect with quanta of light.

  3. 1925–27

    A new mechanics

    Heisenberg and Schrödinger formulate quantum mechanics; Heisenberg states the uncertainty principle.

  4. 1964

    A test for reality

    John Bell shows entanglement can be tested against local hidden variables.

  5. 2022

    A Nobel for entanglement

    Aspect, Clauser and Zeilinger win the Nobel Prize for their entanglement experiments.
